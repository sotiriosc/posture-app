# Exercise Catalog External Evidence Review

Primary research/index records are used only for bounded architecture conclusions; every exercise-specific production claim remains owner-reviewed.

| Evidence | Source | Bounded conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| acsm_progression_models | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11828249/ | Progression and exercise sequencing must be interpreted in the context of target goals, capacity, and training status; no home-equipment-to-beginner inference follows. |
| network_meta_analysis_strength_hypertrophy | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/ | Many resistance prescriptions improve strength/hypertrophy; exercise identity admission still depends on purpose, equipment, and reviewed knowledge. |
| exercise_variation_review | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/ | Variation should be systematic; redundant or frequent random changes do not justify catalog breadth or churn. |
| elastic_resistance_review | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30815258/ | Elastic resistance can support strength, but this does not make every band setup simple, safe, anchored, or load-quantified. |
| machine_free_weight_review | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37582807/ | Machine and free-weight outcomes are modality-specific; selection may follow goals and preferences without declaring one universally safer or superior. |
| task_specificity_review | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40314751/ | Strength transfer is task-specific, supporting truthful identity/path distinctions where the target itself matters. |

The evidence does not prove that every home user is a beginner, one exercise is universally best, familiar exercises are always superior, machines are always safer, or bands are always simpler.
