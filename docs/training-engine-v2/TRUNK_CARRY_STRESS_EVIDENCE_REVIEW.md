# Trunk / Carry Stress Evidence Review

Review date: 2026-08-12

Scope: evidence support for the six approved generic stress concepts. External primary references were discoverable through PubMed/PMC search, but article pages opened through browser-check/reCAPTCHA in this environment. Therefore detailed external review is marked `EXTERNAL_REFERENCE_PENDING` rather than fabricated.

| Concept | Mechanical Definition | Structured Reasoning | Existing Exercise-Domain Support | Owner Judgment | Uncertainty | External Primary Reference Recommended | External Primary Reference Reviewed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `upper_limb_support_loading` | Upper limb bears bodyweight or external support load through hand, forearm, elbow, or shoulder support chain. | Separates support loading from wrist extension and trunk lever demand; support side/duration/lever belong to prescription realization. | Existing support profile can represent floor/wall/bench support; existing wrist tag is too narrow for forearm support. | Accepted as `joint_stress`, side-neutral. | Hand-supported variants and support-regressed variants need reviewed scope. | Yes: plank shoulder/scapular EMG and bodyweight-bearing studies. | `EXTERNAL_REFERENCE_PENDING` |
| `loaded_trunk_rotation` | Controlled trunk rotation against external cable, band, machine, or implement resistance. | Names rotational loading without inferring flexion, extension, overhead pressing, or anti-rotation. | Existing movement role `trunk_rotation` and resistance-path metadata can describe intent/path, but no stress tag existed. | Accepted as `joint_stress`, intrinsic for reviewed resisted-rotation identities. | Range/load and side direction require prescription realization. | Yes: resisted trunk rotation biomechanics/EMG studies. | `EXTERNAL_REFERENCE_PENDING` |
| `lateral_trunk_loading` | Side-bending or anti-lateral-flexion trunk exposure from unilateral load/support or lateral bodyweight support. | Preserves lateral direction and avoids substituting `long_lever_core` or generic carry/core labels. | Existing roles include `anti_lateral_flexion_core`; carry/load laterality exists in prescription contract. | Accepted as `joint_stress`; may be intrinsic or prescription-realized depending on support/load relationship. | Wall support may reduce or remove realized lateral exposure; side compatibility is future prescription behavior. | Yes: side plank, unilateral carry, and frontal-plane trunk loading studies. | `EXTERNAL_REFERENCE_PENDING` |
| `loaded_gait` | Walking while carrying external load; distance, turns, time, load, and side are prescription facts. | Separates walking carry exposure from static load, high impact, and stationary march. | Existing `carry` role and distance/timed carry dose modes support walking truth. | Accepted as `joint_stress`, intrinsic for walking carry identities. | Gait exposure side is often bilateral/systemic while unilateral load side still matters. | Yes: loaded carry, farmer walk, and load-carriage gait biomechanics studies. | `EXTERNAL_REFERENCE_PENDING` |
| `loaded_march` | Stationary stepping or marching while holding external load, without walking distance truth. | Keeps stationary alternating step exposure distinct from loaded walking and distance carry. | Existing step-march dose mode has `stationary: true`, steps/duration, alternation, support, and side behavior. | Accepted as `joint_stress` for wall-supported suitcase march identity. | Support/load/step side interaction requires prescription realization. | Yes: loaded march and step-march lower-limb/gait-control studies. | `EXTERNAL_REFERENCE_PENDING` |
| `grip_loading` | Hand grip is loaded by holding an implement. | Separates ordinary implement holding from `grip_intensive`, which remains dose-created and threshold-pending. | Existing load target and side behavior can record implement side; current `grip_intensive` is overbroad for ordinary carry. | Accepted as `joint_stress`, intrinsic where the reviewed identity requires holding load. | Straps, handles, support, duration, and load can alter or remove realized grip exposure. | Yes: loaded carry grip-demand and handgrip-force studies. | `EXTERNAL_REFERENCE_PENDING` |

External candidates found but not reviewed due access barrier included PubMed/PMC results for plank shoulder/scapular activity, loaded carry muscle activation, strongman/farmer-walk biomechanics, load-carriage gait, and step marching. These search results informed which reference classes are recommended; they were not used as accepted provenance.
