## Sources and bounded conclusions

| Source | Primary conclusion used here |
| --- | --- |
| [PMID_41843416: ACSM Position Stand: Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/) | Many resistance-training forms work; heavier loads and 2-3 sets favor strength, while higher weekly volume favors hypertrophy. Failure, equipment type, time under tension, and complex periodization did not consistently alter outcomes. |
| [PMID_37414459: Resistance training prescription for muscle strength and hypertrophy in healthy adults](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10579494/) | All studied prescriptions outperformed no training; higher-load prescriptions ranked best for strength and multiset prescriptions ranked best for hypertrophy. |
| [PMID_33874848: Influence of resistance training load on skeletal muscle hypertrophy and strength](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33874848/) | Higher and lower loads produced similar hypertrophy, while higher loads produced larger 1RM and isometric strength gains. |
| [PMID_39205815: Give it a rest: inter-set rest interval duration and muscle hypertrophy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/) | Nine randomized studies showed substantial overlap across rest-duration categories; short rest is not a required hypertrophy mechanism. |
| [PMID_28641044: Short versus long inter-set rest intervals and muscle hypertrophy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28641044/) | Both short and long rests can support hypertrophy, with limited evidence suggesting an advantage for longer rests in trained participants. |
| [PMID_25601394: Repetition duration during resistance training and muscle hypertrophy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25601394/) | Hypertrophy was similar across a broad practical repetition-duration range; a single mandatory tempo is not evidence-based and very slow repetitions may be inferior. |
| [PMID_36334240: Resistance training proximity-to-failure and skeletal muscle hypertrophy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9935748/) | Momentary muscular failure was not superior to non-failure training; closer effort can matter without making failure compulsory. |
| [PMID_38970765: Estimated proximity to failure, strength gain, and muscle hypertrophy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/) | Exploratory models found strength gains across a broad RIR range and greater hypertrophy nearer failure, with uncertainty in the exact dose-response. |

## Translation boundary

The evidence supports separate strength and hypertrophy emphases, not rigid universal recipes. Heavy loading is a useful strength-specific signal when equipment and athlete context make it realizable. Hypertrophy can occur across a broad loading range and is more coherently differentiated by sufficient effort and volume. Rest, tempo, and failure are prescription variables, not Product goal labels. These findings concern healthy adults; pain-aware return remains a non-diagnostic context that requires conservative regression and Safety ownership.
